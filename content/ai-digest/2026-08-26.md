### Claude, Anthropic AI Assistant

Anthropic shipped one memory that spans both Claude chat and Claude Cowork, and the pitch is continuity across surfaces: hand Cowork a task and it starts from what Claude already knows from your chats, the project you talked through, your manager's preferences, the client from last quarter. The controls are the interesting half. Everything Claude remembers is saved as a plain list of topics in Settings where you can read, edit, or delete each one individually. ### Andrew Ng, evangelist

Coursera co-founder Andrew Ng shipped a new version of OpenWorker, the open source agent that does not just chat but completes tasks on your laptop, and this release leans hard into security workflows. His reasoning is that after the initial release many users found it especially useful for cybersecurity, and his framing is blunt: attackers are already using AI, so OpenWorker is committed to giving defenders the same leverage. The open source argument is the sharpest part of the pitch. Running an agent requires both a model and a harness, and because the OpenWorker harness is fully open source, security teams can audit it to confirm nobody built in a backdoor that exfiltrates code and data to some company or a foreign adversary. The new build ships built in cybersecurity agents for scanning code for vulnerabilities, scanning dependencies for supply chain injections, and checking cloud security configuration for attack surfaces, all of which pushes security work earlier in the cycle, the shift left idea. Model choice stays yours: run open weight models fully locally so sensitive code never leaves the machine, which also helps with legitimate security work like reproducing a known exploit to defend against it, the kind of task that can trigger refusals in leading closed models. He notes this is joint work with Rohit Prasad. [OpenWorker's security release](https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/2092315079576555806)

Coursera联合创始人Andrew Ng发布了OpenWorker的新版本。这是一个开源agent,它不只是陪你聊天,而是真的在你的笔记本上把任务做完,而这一版重点押在了安全工作流上。他的理由是:首版发布之后,很多用户发现它在网络安全场景下格外好用。他的说法很直接:攻击者已经在用AI了,OpenWorker要做的就是把同等的杠杆交到防守方手里。整套说辞里最锋利的是开源这一条。跑一个agent需要两样东西,一个模型,一个harness,而由于OpenWorker的harness完全开源,安全团队可以自己审计,确认里面没有任何后门把你的代码和数据偷偷送去某家公司,甚至某个境外对手。新版本内置了几个网络安全agent:扫描代码找漏洞、扫描依赖查供应链注入、检查云安全配置排查攻击面,这些都把安全工作往研发周期的更早期推,也就是所谓的"shift left"。模型选择权仍然在你:你可以完全在本地跑开源权重模型,让敏感代码永远不出这台机器,这一点对正当的安全工作也很关键,比如复现一个已知漏洞来做防御,这类任务在头部闭源模型里常常会被拒答。他还提到这是与Rohit Prasad的合作成果。[OpenWorker的安全版本](https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/2092315079576555806)

### Madhu Guru, Meta AI Director

Meta senior AI director Madhu Guru continued his evals series with part 8, on the discriminatory property of evals. The principle: a hill-climbing eval is only useful when it can separate AI systems that are meaningfully different. His example is running an eval across five systems and getting A: 94, B: 93, C: 95, D: 94, E: 92, when you already know A and C are substantially better than D and E. The eval is bad because it does not tell you that, which is low discriminatory power, and he likens it to giving a fifth-grade math test to a group of PhDs, where everyone aces it and you have learned nothing about who is smartest. The correction is not to make evals arbitrarily hard, because then everyone fails and you are no longer measuring what your systems are meant for. The sweet spot he lands on is realistic plus difficult plus sensitive to differences in capability. He ends on the open question worth thinking about: over time your good evals will saturate as your harness and underlying models improve, so what do you do then? [the discriminatory property of evals](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2092058332735693264)

Meta的AI高级总监Madhu Guru更新了evals系列第八篇,主题是eval的"区分度"。原则很简单:一个用来爬坡的eval,只有当它能把有实质差异的AI系统区分开时才有用。他举的例子是:你在五个系统上跑同一个eval,结果是A:94,B:93,C:95,D:94,E:92,而你其实早就知道A和C明显强于D和E。这个eval很糟,因为它压根没告诉你这件事,这就是区分度太低。他打了个比方:这好比让一群博士去做五年级的数学题,人人满分,而你对谁最聪明依然一无所知。但纠正的方式不是把eval做得无限难,那样所有人都挂科,你测的也不再是系统本该胜任的事。他给出的甜点区是:够真实、够难、并且对能力差异足够敏感。他最后抛出了一个值得琢磨的开放问题:随着你的harness和底层模型不断变强,好用的eval迟早会饱和,到那时候你该怎么办?[eval的区分度](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2092058332735693264)

### Peter Yang, AI Educator

AI writer and educator Peter Yang open-sourced /fuck-cancer, a skill that helps patients and caregivers navigate a cancer diagnosis and treatment and advocate for themselves and their loved ones. He built it from what patients and caregivers told him after he shared his mom's story: that you have to be a huge patient advocate and push for answers, biopsies, and proper testing; that people need help navigating difficult conversations; and that the sheer volume of doctors, documents, and insurance paperwork becomes overwhelming fast. The skill creates and maintains a single source of truth brief with five sections: patient and care-team information for easy reference during calls, what to do next limited to three specific actions, what we know with confirmed facts separated from what remains unclear, medical terms explained in plain English, and a care log of recent updates and decisions. It builds the brief from documents and context you provide, and when research is needed it uses trusted sources such as the National Cancer Institute and the ClinicalTrials.gov API. He uses it with ChatGPT, Codex, and Claude Code to prepare for conversations, and it can save the brief locally as Markdown or update a shareable Google Doc so the whole family works from the same information. [open-sourcing the /fuck-cancer skill](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2092249012913258946) · [an example brief the skill produces](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2092311110871617915)

AI作者和教育者Peter Yang开源了一个叫/fuck-cancer的skill,用来帮助患者和照护者应对癌症诊断与治疗,并为自己和家人争取更好的医疗。他做这个东西,是因为在分享了自己母亲的故事之后,患者和照护者告诉他:你必须成为一个特别强势的患者代言人,不停追问、争取活检和该做的检查;人们需要有人帮忙撑过那些艰难的对话;而医生、文件和保险材料的数量之大,很快就会把人压垮。这个skill会创建并持续更新一份作为唯一事实来源的简报,分五个部分:患者与医疗团队信息,方便打电话时随手查;接下来要做什么,只列三件具体的事;我们已知什么,把已确认的事实和仍然不清楚的部分分开;医学术语的大白话解释;以及一份记录近期进展和决定的照护日志。它会从你提供的文档和上下文里生成这份简报,需要查资料时会使用美国国家癌症研究所和ClinicalTrials.gov API等可信来源。他自己会配合ChatGPT、Codex和Claude Code使用它来准备沟通,简报可以存成本地Markdown文件,也可以更新到一个可共享的Google Doc上,让全家人对着同一份信息做决定。[开源/fuck-cancer skill](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2092249012913258946) · [这个skill生成的简报长什么样](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2092311110871617915)
