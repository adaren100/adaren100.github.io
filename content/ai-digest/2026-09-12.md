### Aaron Levie, Box CEO

The meatiest post of the day: Box CEO Aaron Levie met with a couple dozen technology leaders across banking, media, information services, insurance, and consulting, and wrote up what is actually on their minds about agents in the enterprise. Cyber is the top worry, with everyone nervous about the rate of AI-driven vulnerabilities and the implications of the OpenAI Hugging Face incident, though he notes the conversation outside Silicon Valley is less existential and more operational. Most companies are deploying multiple frontier models rather than standardizing on one, open weights remain in their infancy at scale, and agent security and identity management have become live concerns now that agents want into every system. His other themes: the real ROI comes from reengineering the workflow rather than layering agents onto the existing one, architectures are being swapped ruthlessly because nobody waits for a vendor to get it right, evals are still early almost everywhere, and legacy systems remain the perennial drag. Separately, he flagged that you can now mount Box to agent sandboxes so agents can read and write files on their own computer, arguing agents will need the same primitives people have always had. [tales from the road](https://x.com/levie/status/2098218284139311615) · [Box in agent sandboxes](https://x.com/levie/status/2098478938003841123)

今天最有分量的一条:Box CEO Aaron Levie 这周见了二十多位来自银行、媒体、信息服务、保险和咨询行业的技术负责人,然后把他们对企业级 agent 的真实想法整理了出来。排在第一位的担忧是网络安全,大家对 AI 带来的漏洞增长速度以及 OpenAI Hugging Face 事件的影响都很紧张,不过他也提到,硅谷之外的讨论没那么"存亡攸关",更多是在琢磨实际操作层面怎么办。大部分公司同时部署多个前沿模型,而不是统一到某一个上;open weights 在这些组织里的规模化应用还处于非常早期;agent 的安全和身份管理也成了现实问题,毕竟 agent 现在想进的系统越来越多。他的其他几点观察是:真正的 ROI 来自重新设计工作流本身,而不是把 agent 硬贴到现有流程上;架构被更换得非常果断,因为没人愿意等某个厂商慢慢做对;evals 在绝大多数公司还处于起步阶段;遗留系统依然是那个老大难。另外他还提到,现在可以把 Box 挂载到 agent sandbox 里,让 agent 在自己的机器上读写文件,他的观点是:agent 迟早需要人类一直拥有的那套基础能力。[tales from the road](https://x.com/levie/status/2098218284139311615) · [Box 挂载到 agent sandbox](https://x.com/levie/status/2098478938003841123)

### Thariq, Claude Code at Anthropic

Claude Code's Thariq made a pointed observation about benchmarks: it is basically impossible to interpret evals by looking at pass/fail scores alone anymore. Many of the failures he sees come from overly strict hidden tests, and in some cases the model's answer makes more sense than the expected result. He also shipped something aimed at a related pain point, plugin evals, after hearing that people find it hard to tell whether their skills still work after a new model release. You run `claude plugin eval init` in your plugin folder to get started. [on reading evals](https://x.com/trq212/status/2098490139798655427) · [plugin evals](https://x.com/trq212/status/2098531560643539440)

Anthropic Claude Code 团队的 Thariq 对 benchmark 提了一个很尖锐的观察:现在光看 pass/fail 分数,基本已经没法解读 evals 了。他看到的很多"失败"其实来自过于严格的隐藏测试,有些情况下模型给出的答案反而比标准答案更合理。他同时也发布了一个针对相关痛点的功能:plugin evals。起因是有人反馈,新模型发布之后很难判断自己的 skills 是否还能正常工作。在 plugin 目录下运行 `claude plugin eval init` 就能开始用。[怎么读 evals](https://x.com/trq212/status/2098490139798655427) · [plugin evals](https://x.com/trq212/status/2098531560643539440)

### Madhu Guru, Senior Director of AI at Meta

Meta's Senior Director of AI Madhu Guru, who previously led Gemini, Veo, and Nano Banana at Google, laid out why most enterprise AI efforts fail. First, companies run old-school playbooks: the CEO picks a trusted lieutenant to run a central AI team staffed with trusted insiders, but the team structures and product patterns that worked for fifteen years of incremental improvement do not transfer to a field that requires experimentation and invention. Second, they under-invest in evals and do not really understand what good looks like there. Third, the central AI team declares itself a platform team and builds tools from the outside, disconnected from the actual workflows and judgment of the people doing the work, which produces begrudging adoption and no real productivity gain. His prescription: hire leaders who have actually shipped AI products, make evals a first class citizen, and embed your best AI builders inside the functions you want to transform, building with finance, with sales, with support rather than for them. [why enterprise AI fails](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2098448235048378456)

Meta AI 高级总监 Madhu Guru(此前在 Google 带过 Gemini、Veo 和 Nano Banana)讲了为什么大多数企业的 AI 项目都会失败。第一,还在用老一套打法:CEO 挑一个信得过的副手去带一个中心化的 AI 团队,再从公司各处拉一批信得过的人进来。问题是技能断层,过去十五年做渐进式改进所形成的团队结构、产品模式、发布和迭代方式,在 AI 这件事上并不适用,因为 AI 需要的是实验和发明。第二,在 evals 上投入严重不足,而且大多数企业并不真正理解"做得好"是什么样子。第三,中心化的 AI 团队把自己定位成平台团队,从外部造工具让所有人来用,但这些产品往往脱离一线真实的工作流、上下文和判断,结果就是勉强被用起来,却没带来实质的效率提升。他给的三条解法是:招那些真正做成过 AI 产品的负责人,既要有技术和产品深度,也要能赢得那些"工作方式正在被改造"的人的信任;把 evals 当成一等公民;把最好的 AI builder 直接派驻到你想改造的业务线里,和财务一起做、和销售一起做、和客服一起做,而不是替他们做。[why enterprise AI fails](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2098448235048378456)

### Peter Yang, AI Writer and Creator

Peter Yang came out skeptical of software factories. Outside of verification and testing, he does not think AI is at a point where it can self-improve a product or build a new feature end to end without a human in the loop: whenever he loops something overnight to build something new, one wrong assumption turns the whole run into wasted tokens. He put the challenge directly to his readers, asking what products or features have actually been built end to end with a software factory, with no human defining the requirements or checking the work. In a separate post he noted that Sol has become more hesitant to just get things done, responding to "make updates now" with "I can make updates, but please confirm," even for simple document edits, and wondered whether it is a harness or default prompt change. He filed a feedback ticket. [skeptical of software factories](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2098565668241334366) · [Sol asking for confirmation](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2098557610089959910)

Peter Yang 明确表示他对"software factory"持怀疑态度。他认为除了验证和测试之外,AI 目前还做不到在没有人参与的情况下自我改进产品、端到端地实现一个新功能:每次他让模型通宵跑一个新东西,只要其中有一个假设错了,整轮下来就是在烧 token。他直接把问题抛了出来:到底有哪些产品或功能,是完全由 software factory 端到端做出来的,全程没有人定义需求、也没有人检查结果?另一条帖子里他提到,Sol 变得越来越不愿意"直接动手"了,他说"现在就更新",得到的回复却是"我可以更新,但请先确认",而这只是改一个普通文档而已。他猜测可能是 harness 或者默认 prompt 改动导致的,并且已经提交了反馈。[对 software factory 的怀疑](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2098565668241334366) · [Sol 变得爱反复确认](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2098557610089959910)

### Nikunj Kothari, FPV Ventures Partner

FPV Ventures partner Nikunj Kothari opened with "Success has many fathers, but failure is an orphan," and said he never grasped the weight of it until he watched extremely successful VCs fight over attribution on hot deals. The stakes are real because large exits and markups are genuinely rare, and those are what you raise your next fund on. His prediction: over the next couple of years, expect names to get scrubbed or conveniently omitted as people revise history to claim credit for the winners. Emerging GPs feel this most acutely since track record is the only thing they can take to future LPs, and his advice to them is to hold their founders close, because founders are the real reference checks. [attribution in venture](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2098550718923997430)

FPV Ventures 合伙人 Nikunj Kothari 引用了一句话开头:"成功有很多父亲,失败却是个孤儿。"他说自己一直到亲眼看见那些非常成功的 VC 为热门项目的"归属权"争来争去,才真正体会到这句话的分量。之所以争得厉害,是因为大额退出和估值上涨本身就很稀有,而这恰恰是你募下一期基金的底气所在。他的判断是:未来几年会看到不少名字被抹掉或者"恰好省略",大家都想重写历史,给最终跑出来的项目认领一份功劳。新兴 GP 对此感受最深,因为他们能拿给未来 LP 看的只有 track record。他给这些人的建议是:和你的创始人保持紧密关系,因为他们才是真正的 reference check。[attribution in venture](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2098550718923997430)

### Matt Turck, FirstMark VC

FirstMark VC Matt Turck republished a lightly updated version of his 9/11 story, originally written in 2016. In September 2001 he was the young co-founder of TripleHop Technologies, a roughly 25 person NYC startup building enterprise search with whatever AI existed at the time, close to what Glean does today. Their office was on the 53rd floor of the North Tower, chosen over a larger space around the 104th floor because a seed-funded startup taking the top of the building felt like pushing it. He was home when the plane hit, and it eventually turned out only one team member, Kenton Beerman, was in the office at 8:46am; he got out unscathed. The team spent the day trying to save the business, and because CTO Joaquin Delgado's team had backed everything up from their own server room, they were essentially running again by end of day. TripleHop survived the nuclear winter that followed on a $5M Series A raised months before, and was acquired by Oracle in 2005. He visited the memorial for the first time only a few weeks ago, and wrote that looking at the names, he was reminded how little it would have taken: an hour later, a few floors lower, or the other office. [my 9/11 story](https://x.com/mattturck/status/2098517022049190227)

FirstMark 的 VC Matt Turck 重新发布了他的 9/11 回忆,这是他 2016 年写的博客的小幅修订版。2001 年 9 月,他是纽约创业公司 TripleHop Technologies 的年轻联合创始人,团队大约 25 人,用当时能用上的 AI 技术做企业搜索,大致相当于今天的 Glean。他们的办公室在北塔 53 层。当初他们其实也看过 104 层附近的一间更大的办公室,视野极好,但对一家刚拿种子轮的公司来说,占据大楼顶层实在有点过了,而且电梯要坐很久,于是选了更小更便宜的 53 层。飞机撞上大楼时他正在家里。后来确认,8 点 46 分那一刻只有一位同事 Kenton Beerman 在办公室,他惊魂未定但毫发无伤。那一天团队几乎把全部精力放在抢救业务上,幸好 CTO Joaquin Delgado 和他的团队把自建机房里的数据都备份过,到当天结束时系统基本恢复运转。之后是互联网泡沫破裂叠加 9/11 带来的漫长寒冬,靠着事发前几个月融到的 500 万美元 A 轮,TripleHop 撑了过来,并在 2005 年被 Oracle 收购。他说自己很少谈起 9/11,也一直尽量不去多想,直到几周前开完董事会路过,才第一次走进 9/11 纪念馆。看着大理石上刻着的名字,他意识到当年那道界线有多细:晚一个小时,低几层楼,或者几个月前选了 104 层的那间办公室,结局就完全不同。[my 9/11 story](https://x.com/mattturck/status/2098517022049190227)
