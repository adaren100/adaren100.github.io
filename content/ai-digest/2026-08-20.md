That gap between the falling cost of shipping software and the rising cost of the tokens it takes to write it is the thing the two companies say they are fixing together. ### OpenAI

OpenAI is previewing Private Safety Processing, an approach designed to improve safety without giving OpenAI personnel access to the underlying content. The company also confirmed it will continue to offer Zero Data Retention for frontier models. The reasoning behind it: as AI takes on longer and more autonomous work for businesses, safety systems increasingly need to spot risks that only show up across related interactions, not within a single one. [Private Safety Processing](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2090165328290701800)

OpenAI正在预览Private Safety Processing,这套做法的目标是在不让OpenAI员工接触到具体内容的前提下,把安全性做上去。公司同时确认,前沿模型会继续提供Zero Data Retention。背后的逻辑是:当AI替企业承担的任务链条越来越长、自主性越来越高,安全系统就越来越需要识别那些跨多次交互才暴露出来的风险,而不是只看单次对话。[Private Safety Processing](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2090165328290701800)

### Aaron Levie, Box CEO

Box CEO Aaron Levie laid out the most substantive argument of the day: the value that can be created between the AI model and the end-user workflow is far larger than most people assumed. He walked through six reasons the applied AI layer stays defensible. Agents have to show up differently depending on the business process, sometimes as chat, sometimes as a background agent inside a deterministic workflow. Different workflows plug into entirely different enterprise systems and data, and working with life sciences, financial or legal data each demands its own contextual approach. Domain-specific change management still matters, because implementing technology at a bank looks nothing like doing it at a law firm. Model flexibility lets you tune workflows across cost and performance tiers, and eventually post-train for specific tasks. Evals are essential, and domain-specific ones have a long tail that no single system can cover. And many verticals need pricing models that abstract above raw tokens. [six reasons the applied AI layer is defensible](https://x.com/levie/status/2089921630650925170)

Box首席执行官Aaron Levie给出了今天最有分量的一段论述:在AI模型和最终用户工作流之间,能创造出来的价值远比大多数人以为的要大。他列了六条理由,说明应用层为什么守得住。第一,agent在不同业务流程里的呈现方式完全不同,有时候是聊天界面,有时候是跑在确定性流程里的后台agent。第二,不同工作流对接的是完全不同的企业系统和数据,处理生命科学、金融或法律数据,各自都需要自己那一套上下文方案。第三,分领域的变革管理依然关键,在银行落地技术和在律所落地完全是两回事。第四,能在多个模型之间切换,意味着你可以按成本和性能分档去调优工作流,后面还能针对具体任务做post-train。第五,evals不可或缺,而领域专属的evals有极长的长尾,没有哪一套系统能全覆盖。第六,不少垂直行业需要的定价模型,得在原始token之上再抽象一层。[应用层守得住的六条理由](https://x.com/levie/status/2089921630650925170)

He also flagged the Stripe and OpenRouter deal as an important piece of the same picture. For AI to diffuse more broadly, developers and enterprises need to mix and match intelligence from a variety of providers seamlessly, and manage costs while doing it. [on Stripe and OpenRouter](https://x.com/levie/status/2090137914785280189)

他还点了一下Stripe和OpenRouter的这笔交易,认为它是同一幅图景里的重要一块。要让AI扩散得更广,开发者和企业需要能在不同供应商之间无缝地组合调用智能,同时把成本管住。[关于Stripe和OpenRouter](https://x.com/levie/status/2090137914785280189)

### Thariq, Claude Code at Anthropic

Thariq, who works on Claude Code at Anthropic, made the case for the software factory by starting with an uncomfortable fact: for its entire existence, creating software has been an unreliable endeavor. Most projects ran late, went over budget, and still missed user needs, and if you were an SMB you simply could not get good software built for you. Building net new software products, on the other hand, will stay unreliable and risky, and profitable precisely because of that. ### Madhu Guru, Senior Director of AI at Meta

Madhu Guru, Senior Director of AI at Meta and previously the lead on Gemini, Veo and Nano Banana at Google, offered the cleanest heuristic of the day for eval spending: treat evals like frontier models. Establish the quality frontier first, then work your way down the cost curve. In practice that means writing the rubric before anything else, deciding how to measure it (humans, LLM judge, or automated verification), and then actually spending the money at that stage. Use the expensive judge model, pay the humans, run the best process you can, so you get a signal you trust. Only once the eval reliably separates good from bad and reflects what you care about should you optimize: more automation, smaller judge models, sampling, deterministic checks. Quality first, cost next. He says this is a series and is taking questions. [quality first, cost next](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2089918106814603728)

Meta的AI高级总监Madhu Guru,此前在Google带过Gemini、Veo和Nano Banana,给出了今天关于eval投入最干净的一条判断准则:把evals当成前沿模型来做。先把质量的上限立住,再顺着成本曲线往下走。具体做法是:第一步先写rubric,然后决定怎么测量,人工、LLM judge还是自动化验证,接着在这个阶段该花的钱一定要花。用贵的judge模型,给人工评估付钱,把流程跑到你能做到的最好,这样拿到的信号你才敢信。只有当这套eval能稳定区分好坏、并且真的反映你在产品上关心的东西之后,再去优化成本:加自动化、换更小的judge模型、做采样、能用确定性检查的地方就用。质量第一,成本第二。他说这是个系列,欢迎提问。[质量第一,成本第二](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2089918106814603728)
