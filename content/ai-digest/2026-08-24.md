### Guillermo Rauch, Vercel CEO

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch made the cleanest economic argument of the day: intelligence is getting cheaper, and the numbers show it. OpenAI Sol's price cuts, combined with discounts on Vercel AI Gateway, have made Sol their fastest-growing frontier model. He draws two conclusions from that. First, demand for intelligence is highly elastic, so as inference costs fall, usage grows rapidly. Second, if you are not routing through a gateway, you are missing out on this price volatility, which is what lowers your operating costs and lifts your margins. His read on the market that follows: it is no wonder the router space has heated up, gateways are inevitable. [intelligence is getting cheaper](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2091671326897713424)

Vercel首席执行官Guillermo Rauch给出了今天最干净利落的一笔经济账:智能正在变便宜,而且数据摆在那儿。OpenAI Sol降价,叠加Vercel AI Gateway上的折扣,直接把Sol推成了他们增长最快的前沿模型。他从中读出两件事。第一,对智能的需求弹性极高,推理成本一降,用量就会迅速涨上来。第二,如果你没有走gateway,你就吃不到这波价格波动的红利,而正是这波红利在压低你的运营成本、抬高你的利润率。顺着这个逻辑,他对市场的判断是:router赛道打得这么热一点都不奇怪,gateway是必然的。[智能正在变便宜](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2091671326897713424)

He also laid out Vercel's philosophy for extending fx, and it comes down to open protocols: MCP, Skills and Plugins. But the best protocol, in his telling, is still Unix, and he means two specific properties. One, small programs that do one thing well and compose by calling other programs. Two, libfx, which makes fx embeddable inside more complex programs, so you should be able to build your own CLI, your own background agent, your own software factory, running local or in the cloud. [extending fx with open protocols](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2091583525661384813)

他还讲了Vercel扩展fx的思路,核心就是开放协议:MCP、Skills和Plugins。但在他看来,最好的那个协议依然是Unix,而且他指的是两个很具体的特性。一是小程序只把一件事做好,靠互相调用来组合。二是libfx,它让fx能嵌进更复杂的程序里,所以你应该有能力搭自己的CLI、自己的后台agent、自己的软件工厂,本地跑或者云上跑都行。[用开放协议扩展fx](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2091583525661384813)

### Aaron Levie, Box CEO

Box CEO Aaron Levie argued that AI diffusion is far more rate limited by having good evals than most people realize. The evals you see attached to every model release are genuinely helpful, but he points out what they actually measure: the shape of general AI progress and the relative capability level of models, and not much beyond that. The far bigger space over time, he says, is evals on all the major workflows enterprises actually run, down to the specifics of an individual company. His reasoning for why this becomes a huge category: you cannot automate what you cannot assess the progress on, and enterprises will not be able to go just on vibes. [evals are the real rate limiter](https://x.com/levie/status/2091359223368315050)

Box首席执行官Aaron Levie认为,AI的扩散速度受制于evals的程度,远超大多数人的想象。每次模型发布时附带的那套evals确实有用,但他点出了它们真正衡量的东西:AI整体进展的形态,以及模型之间的相对能力水平,再往外就没多少了。他说,长期来看真正大的那块空间,是针对企业实际在跑的各条主要工作流做evals,细到某一家公司的具体情况。至于这为什么会长成一个巨大的品类,他的理由是:你没法自动化一件你无法评估进展的事,而企业是不可能只凭感觉往前走的。[evals才是真正的速度瓶颈](https://x.com/levie/status/2091359223368315050)

### Peter Yang, AI Tutorials and Podcast Host

Peter Yang, who makes practical AI tutorials and interviews, surfaced a distinction worth keeping from his conversation with Shreya Shankar: there are two types of AI evals, top down and bottom up. Her framing of the first is, if you are in a vacuum and given just the task description, what would you come up with, and she says Claude does a very good job of helping you with those. Bottom up evals are the other half, where you look at lots and lots of sample outputs and ask what your gut feedback is that you want externalized into evals. Her verdict on that half is blunt: Claude is very, very bad at coming up with bottom up evals, and that part is all you. [top down versus bottom up evals](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2091586298779955512)

做AI实用教程和访谈的Peter Yang,从他和Shreya Shankar的对话里拎出了一个值得记住的区分:AI evals分两种,自上而下和自下而上。她对第一种的描述是:假设你处在真空里,手上只有一份任务描述,你会想出什么来?她说Claude在这件事上帮得很到位。自下而上的evals则是另一半:你去看大量大量的样本输出,然后问自己,心里那份直觉判断该怎么外化成evals。她对这后一半的评价很直接:Claude在想自下而上的evals这件事上非常非常差,这部分全得靠你自己。[自上而下 vs 自下而上的evals](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2091586298779955512)

### Nikunj Kothari, FPV Ventures Partner

FPV Ventures partner Nikunj Kothari posted the bluntest founder advice of the day, after being on the receiving end of the behavior: ragebaiting investors and then sending them SAFE docs to just wire you money is not how investing works. [ragebaiting investors is not how it works](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2091381756012511244)

FPV Ventures的合伙人Nikunj Kothari发了今天对创始人最不留情面的一条建议,而且他本人就是被这么对待的那一方:先激怒投资人,然后甩一份SAFE文件过去让人家直接打钱,投资不是这么做的。[激怒投资人不是这么玩的](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2091381756012511244)

He followed up with three clarifications. These were Twitter DMs, and he tries to be helpful to as many cold DMs as he can, but this one should not have passed the bar. Doxxing someone is not his style, and he hopes they learned their lesson. And the part he says he cannot stress enough: it matters enormously who you take advice from. What boils his blood is watching talented young people who actually have substance play these stupid games. [find the right people to take advice from](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2091403643815604629)

他随后补了三点。第一,这些都是Twitter私信,他尽量对每一封陌生私信都给点实际帮助,但这封确实没达到那个门槛。第二,人肉挂人不是他的风格,他希望对方吸取教训。第三,也是他说怎么强调都不为过的一点:你从谁那里听建议,这件事极其重要。真正让他窝火的,是看到那些本身有真才实学的年轻人,却跑去玩这种没意义的把戏。[找对能给你建议的人](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2091403643815604629)

### Peter Steinberger, OpenClaw and OpenAI

Peter Steinberger, who works on OpenClaw and at OpenAI, made a short case against CLI purism: the CLI is nice, but having UI visualizations and your team where you work is nicer. [CLI is nice, UI plus your team is nicer](https://x.com/steipete/status/2091650136506327253)

在OpenClaw和OpenAI两边做事的Peter Steinberger,简短地反驳了一下CLI原教旨主义:CLI是不错,但有UI可视化、而且团队就在你干活的地方,那更好。[CLI不错,但UI加上团队更好](https://x.com/steipete/status/2091650136506327253)

He also shipped a small hardware integration: he added the rotation USB protocol to OpenClaw and told his claw to look around, and it is now having fun with his 360 webcam. [teaching the claw to look around](https://x.com/steipete/status/2091639468935831910)

他还做了个小的硬件打通:给OpenClaw加上了旋转USB协议,然后让他的claw四处看看,现在它正拿着他那个360摄像头玩得不亦乐乎。[教claw四处张望](https://x.com/steipete/status/2091639468935831910)

### Ryo Lu, Product Designer

Ryo Lu, the designer behind work at Cursor, Notion and Stripe, posted something entirely different from the rest of the timeline: a poem called leaving, which he describes as the process from fullness into emptiness. It moves through what gets left behind, the familiar circle of life, the routines undone, the streets he knew by heart, the names and labels that had gathered on his skin, toward a place entirely unknown where a new life waits to be written. The turn in the middle is the part that lands: when he first arrived he was drifting in a river, carried by the current, thinking only of making things, and now he has to place his own hands on the helm, no longer mistaking the waves for fate or the shore for salvation. What he says he has to learn again is how to sleep inside loneliness, how to eat inside uncertainty, and how to turn freedom from an idea into a life he practices every day. [leaving](https://x.com/ryolu_/status/2091596272654582062)

在Cursor、Notion和Stripe都留下过设计作品的Ryo Lu,发了一条和整条timeline气质完全不同的东西:一首叫《leaving》的诗,他把离开描述成一个从充盈走向空无的过程。诗里一件件数着被留下的东西:熟悉的生活轨迹、被拆掉的日常、闭着眼都能走的街道、这些年累积在皮肤上的名字和标签,然后走向一个全然陌生的地方,那里有一段等着被写下的新生活。中间那个转折最打动人:刚来的时候,他是漂在河里的,被水流推着走,脑子里只想着做点东西出来;而现在,他得把手放到舵上,不再把浪当成命运,也不再把岸当成救赎。他说自己要重新学会的,是怎么在孤独里睡着,怎么在不确定里吃饭,以及怎么把自由从一个概念,变成每天都在过的生活。[leaving](https://x.com/ryolu_/status/2091596272654582062)
