### AI at Meta, Meta's AI Research Account

Meta released Muse Spark 1.3, rolling out in Muse Code and the Meta Model API, with max reasoning to follow once safety testing finishes. What makes the release note unusually credible is that the headline capabilities are about behavior rather than benchmark deltas: it sustains longer-horizon work across multiple workflows in a single thread, it asks clarifying questions and flags when it is stuck and confirms before consequential actions, and it is better calibrated on its own limits instead of hallucinating outcomes.

Meta发布了Muse Spark 1.3,正在Muse Code和Meta Model API上线,max reasoning版本会在安全测试完成后跟上。这份发布说明之所以格外可信,是因为它主打的能力讲的是行为而不是benchmark涨点:它能在单个会话里跨多个工作流维持更长周期的任务,它会主动提出澄清问题、在卡住时明确说自己卡住了、在做有后果的操作前先确认,而且对自己能力边界的校准更准,不会硬编一个结果出来。

The efficiency numbers are the ones to file away: roughly 20 percent fewer tool calls and roughly 25 percent fewer tokens versus Muse Spark 1.2 in internal comparisons. For anyone running agents at volume that is a cost line, not a nice-to-have. Meta also flagged that bigger models and Muse Spark open weights are coming. ### Sebastian Raschka, Evangelist

The most valuable technical post of the day is a debunk. ML researcher and author Sebastian Raschka pushed back on the hype around OpenAI's Astra model, which The Information described as a recurrent depth or looped transformer. His point: the looped transformer idea is just reusing layers in the transformer block, and it is not new. He pointed to Nanbeige 4.2, whose 3B model was pretrained from scratch on 28T tokens using exactly this, reusing a 22-layer stack twice to effectively get 44 layers without duplicating weights.

当天最有价值的技术帖是一次辟谣。ML研究者、作者Sebastian Raschka对OpenAI Astra模型引发的热度泼了盆冷水,The Information把它描述成recurrent depth或者looped transformer。他的观点是:looped transformer这个思路无非就是复用transformer block里的层,而且一点都不新。他举了Nanbeige 4.2的例子,那个3B模型就是从头用28T token预训练的,做法完全一样:把22层的stack复用两次,在不复制权重的前提下等效拿到44层。

The trade-off is clean once you see it. Roughly double the model size in effect, same storage and RAM footprint, but almost 2x the compute, because you push the embedded text through nearly 2x the layers. Nanbeige's researchers found two passes gave the best trade-off and retained about 75 percent of the token efficiency of a standard architecture, with more passes buying almost nothing while making training much slower and more expensive. The idea traces back to the NeurIPS paper on mixture-of-recursions, which goes further with a learned router deciding per token how many passes it gets, so easy tokens exit early and hard ones get more computation.

一旦看明白,这个取舍其实很干净:等效上模型大了近一倍,存储和内存占用不变,但计算量差不多翻倍,因为文本要过将近两倍的层数。Nanbeige的研究者发现两次pass是最优取舍,能保留标准架构约75%的token效率;继续增加pass几乎带不来收益,反而让训练明显更慢更贵。这个思路可以追溯到NeurIPS上那篇mixture-of-recursions的论文,它走得更远,加了一个学习出来的router来决定每个token走几次pass,简单的token提前退出,难的token拿到更多计算。

He also took apart the claim that the technique obscures the model's chain-of-thought. Reusing layers does not by itself suppress visible reasoning; it adds computation in hidden states before the next token is emitted, exactly as ordinary transformer layers do. The only plausible reading, he argues, is that more recurrent passes may mean fewer intermediate reasoning tokens need to be generated, pushing more computation into latent activations that cannot be read as text, but you would get the same effect simply by scaling model size. He notes the journalist may have been referring to a different technique or misunderstood this one. A video version of the explainer followed. [debunking the Astra looped transformer hype](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2095141254958858496) · [the YouTube version](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2095288516309569644)

他还拆解了"这个技术会遮蔽模型chain-of-thought"的说法。复用层本身并不会压制可见的推理过程,它只是在下一个token生成之前在隐藏状态里增加计算,和普通transformer层做的事完全一样。他认为唯一说得通的解读是:更多的recurrent pass可能意味着需要生成的中间推理token变少,于是更多计算发生在无法被读成文字的latent activation里,但你单纯把模型放大也会得到同样的效果。他还指出,那位记者可能指的是另一种技术,或者误解了这一种。之后他还发了这个讲解的视频版。[拆解Astra looped transformer的热度](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2095141254958858496) · [YouTube版本](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2095288516309569644)

### Madhu Guru, Senior Director of AI at Meta

Meta Senior Director of AI Madhu Guru, who previously led Gemini, Veo and Nano Banana at Google, made the clearest product argument of the day: every AI product should be working towards abstracting model choice away from users. The reasoning is that in any product the intelligence is the underlying engine, all the user cares about is that the job gets done, and model picking is too much cognitive load to push onto them.

Meta的AI高级总监Madhu Guru之前在Google带过Gemini、Veo和Nano Banana,他提出了当天最清晰的产品主张:每个AI产品都该朝着"把选模型这件事从用户面前拿掉"的方向走。理由是,在任何产品里,智能都只是底层引擎,用户唯一在乎的是活儿有没有干成,而让用户去挑模型,是把过重的认知负担丢给了他们。

He acknowledges there have been multiple attempts at this before and thinks we are finally at the point where it can work, but only with three things in place: deep understanding of the model frontier across weight classes for your specific use cases, great evals for your workflows so you know which models work best where, and a team with the talent and AI-native operating model to keep adapting as the frontier moves. He credits the Lovable team with having all three. In a related post he pointed at Shopify's ML team as an example of what enterprises gain by building their own post-training systems, strong evals and a data flywheel, and argued more enterprises should be doing it. [abstracting model choice away from users](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2095174463696589223) · [enterprises building their own post-training and data flywheel](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2094973690576576675)

他承认这件事之前有过多次尝试,并认为现在终于到了能做成的时候,但前提是三件事同时具备:对你具体用例下、不同参数量级的模型前沿有深入理解;针对你的工作流有好的eval,知道哪个模型在哪里最强;以及一支有能力、有AI原生运作方式的团队,能随着前沿的移动持续调整。他认为Lovable团队三样都有。在另一条帖子里,他以Shopify的ML团队为例,说明企业自建post-training体系、优质eval和数据飞轮能获得什么,并认为更多企业都该这么做。[把选模型从用户面前拿掉](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2095174463696589223) · [企业自建post-training与数据飞轮](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2094973690576576675)

### Aditya Agarwal, General Partner at SPC

SPC general partner and Bevel Health co-founder Aditya Agarwal, formerly an early Facebook engineer and Dropbox CTO, made the most uncomfortable point of the day: it is actually really hard to trust frontier labs when they say they will not use your data for training, which he sees as a huge reason to use open source models. His own summary of the irony is that we now live in a world where you use Chinese models to make sure your stuff is private, which is exactly why he thinks US open source is critical.

SPC的普通合伙人、Bevel Health联合创始人Aditya Agarwal,早年是Facebook的早期工程师、Dropbox的CTO,他提出了当天最让人不舒服的一点:当前沿实验室说"放心,我们不会拿你的数据训练"时,你其实很难真的信,而这正是使用开源模型的一个重要理由。他自己对这个反讽的总结是:我们现在活在一个"用中国的模型来确保自己数据隐私"的世界里,而这恰恰说明美国的开源为什么至关重要。

He also had advice for founders that cuts against how most AI startups are built: too many are focused on solving model issues, or leveraging model capabilities, as they exist today. He thinks it is much more interesting to hold a strong point of view on what capabilities and issues will look like a year out, while admitting that is understandably hard. [trusting labs on training data, and why US open source matters](https://x.com/adityaag/status/2095227334534041714) · [build for the frontier a year from now](https://x.com/adityaag/status/2095192873973301601)
