### Madhu Guru, Sr. Director of AI at Meta

Meta Sr. Director of AI Madhu Guru made a direct call on where the alpha in AI products sits over the next few years: the application layer. His reasoning is that models will keep getting cheaper, better and more local, so the differentiation has to come from deeply understanding specific user workflows and having the imagination to redesign the experience around them. The sting in the tail: the denominator of people who can build AI products is about to get enormous, which is exactly why being in the top 0.1% of builders will matter more than ever. He made a related point about distribution, arguing that dev rels are having their moment because building software has become trivial, so people who combine a great social media game with real technical chops are invaluable. ### Aaron Levie, Box CEO

Box CEO Aaron Levie argues that forward deployed engineers are real and are not going away for AI any time soon, and that the reason this is happening at a scale never seen before is that AI fundamentally adds a non-deterministic, rapidly changing system to workflows that have largely never been automated before. This looks very little like implementing traditional software: software has always been mostly deterministic, so the upfront implementation work was relatively uniform across similar customers and the system was not regularly being upgraded in fundamental ways. He quotes the line that captures the difference: if you are building an AI agent for accounting in 2026, there is no established workflow, because literally nobody has ever used one of these, and customers cannot tell you what they want because the thing they would want does not have a shape yet. [why FDEs are not going away](https://x.com/levie/status/2087385493684335064)

Box首席执行官Aaron Levie认为,forward deployed engineer(FDE)这个角色是真实存在的需求,在AI时代短期内不会消失。之所以现在会出现前所未有的规模,根本原因在于AI往那些几乎从未被自动化过的工作流里,塞进了一个非确定性的、还在快速变化的系统。这跟实施传统软件几乎不是一回事:传统软件基本是确定性的,所以对相似客户来说,前期实施工作相对标准化,系统本身也不会隔三差五就来一次根本性升级。他引用了一句话来概括这种差别:如果你在2026年做一个记账场景的AI agent,根本不存在什么现成的工作流,因为压根就没人用过这种东西;客户也说不清自己想要什么,因为他们想要的那个东西还没成形。[FDE为什么不会消失](https://x.com/levie/status/2087385493684335064)

Levie adds that AI agents are different on nearly every dimension: the customer's business process has to change to work with agents, heavy customization is necessary to get agents working in the customer's end-state process, evals need to be run constantly, the models keep changing and updates need to keep being incorporated, and the underlying harness and broader system often shift with customer feedback. His conclusion is that this is real work for customers, systems integrators and applied AI vendors alike, and that even as AI capabilities improve dramatically the work remains or gets more complicated, because enterprises will simply throw increasingly complex processes at agents. Great time to be an FDE.

Levie还补充说,AI agent几乎在每个维度上都跟传统软件不一样:客户的业务流程本身必须为配合agent而改变;要让agent在客户的最终流程里真正跑通,大量定制是必需的;eval需要持续不断地跑;模型一直在变,更新得不停地往里合;底层harness和整个系统也常常因为客户反馈而调整。他的结论是,这对客户、系统集成商和应用层AI厂商来说都是实打实的活儿;而且就算AI能力大幅提升,这些活儿也不会消失,甚至会更复杂,因为企业只会把越来越复杂的流程一股脑丢给agent。所以现在正是做FDE的好时候。

### Google DeepMind

Google DeepMind introduced SL2T, a sign language translation model, and explained why this problem has resisted progress for years: complex technical challenges plus persistent misconceptions about how sign languages actually work. Their approach was to train a model to translate the simultaneous movements of the hands, body and face directly into text, using huge amounts of data. SL2T is state of the art on academic benchmarks and is also optimized for real-world use such as one-handed signing while holding a phone, and to protect privacy it tracks body poses on-device while the servers translate those poses into text. The team built it with the Deaf community, guided by Deaf Googlers and their AI Sign Language Advisory Committee, and says bringing ASL input to phones is just the beginning, with work underway to expand to more sign languages and applications. [why this has been hard for years](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2087541215323476034) · [benchmarks and on-device privacy](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2087541217965809850) · [built with the Deaf community](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2087541220494938537)

Google DeepMind推出了手语翻译模型SL2T,并解释了这个问题为什么多年来进展缓慢:一方面是技术本身足够复杂,另一方面是人们对手语的运作方式长期存在误解。他们的做法是,用海量数据训练一个模型,把手部、身体和面部的同步动作直接翻译成文本。SL2T在学术benchmark上达到了state of the art,同时也针对真实使用场景做了优化,比如单手打手语、另一只手拿着手机的情况;在隐私方面,身体姿态的追踪在设备本地完成,服务器只负责把姿态数据翻译成文本。这个项目是与聋人社群共同打造的,由Google内部的聋人员工以及公司的AI手语顾问委员会提供指导。团队表示,把ASL输入带到手机上只是个开始,他们正在推进对更多手语和更多应用场景的支持。[这件事为什么难了这么多年](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2087541215323476034) · [benchmark表现与端侧隐私设计](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2087541217965809850) · [与聋人社群共同打造](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2087541220494938537)
