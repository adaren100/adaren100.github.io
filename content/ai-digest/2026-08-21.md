### Guillermo Rauch, Vercel CEO

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch wrote the most technical post of the day, about fx: 6.3mb, starts up in 10 microseconds, shipped as a Zig-compiled static ELF binary or an even smaller libfx.wasm. His argument is that AI will make most infrastructure natively optimized, and fx is the case in point, because it can literally finish tasks faster than other agents can boot. His line for it: fast is a one-way street. The footnote is the interesting part, though. The wasm build is actually smaller than the native one, because a lot of binary weight comes from the TLS/HTTP stack, and in WebAssembly fx delegates fetch() to the JS runtime instead. [fx starts up in 10µs](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2090255740384751664)

Vercel首席执行官Guillermo Rauch发了今天技术含量最高的一条,讲的是fx:6.3mb,启动只要10微秒,编译产物是Zig编出来的静态ELF binary,或者体积更小的libfx.wasm。他想说的是,AI会让大部分基础设施都变成原生优化过的形态,而fx就是活例子:它干完活的速度,比别的agent启动还快。他给的定语是:快是一条单行道。不过真正有意思的是脚注那部分。wasm版本居然比原生版本还小,因为二进制体积里很大一块来自TLS/HTTP协议栈,而在WebAssembly里,fx把fetch()直接交给JS runtime去做了。[fx启动只要10µs](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2090255740384751664)

### Aaron Levie, Box CEO

Box CEO Aaron Levie weighed in on the expert versus generalist debate, and his read is that so far the experts have the upper hand, and that is not slowing down. His reasoning: AI makes it 10X easier to get started with any kind of task, whether coding, legal work, research or financial analysis. But having the judgment to direct the agent onto the right work, to course correct it, to review or test the output, and to know what good actually looks like still requires a high degree of domain skill. AI does let anyone build that skill far faster, which he calls a genuine uplift for careers. His conclusion is the uncomfortable one, though: AI will exacerbate differences in skill levels, precisely because experts now have far more leverage than ever. Net net, don't give up on being an expert at something. [experts still have the upper hand](https://x.com/levie/status/2090278256306229675)

Box首席执行官Aaron Levie聊了聊AI时代做专才还是做通才的争论,他的判断是:到目前为止专家占上风,而且这个趋势没有放缓的迹象。他的理由是,AI确实让任何一件事的入门难度降了10倍,写代码、做法务、做研究、做财务分析都一样。但要判断该把agent派去干哪件事、中途怎么把它拽回正轨、怎么审阅和验证产出、以及心里清楚什么才叫做得好,这些依然需要相当深的领域功力。AI的好处是让任何有心人都能更快地把这份功力练出来,他认为这对职业发展是实打实的提升。不过他的结论有点扎心:AI会把技能水平的差距进一步拉大,原因恰恰是专家现在手里的杠杆比以往任何时候都大。总之一句话,别放弃在某件事上做到专家。[专家依然占上风](https://x.com/levie/status/2090278256306229675)

### Madhu Guru, Meta Sr Director of AI

Madhu Guru, Senior Director of AI at Meta, continued his eval series with two of the most practical posts of the week. Part three is about building a failure modes taxonomy, which he says should be the first thing you build once you have v1 of your evals. Start from production traces, study the last 500 to 1,000 interactions, look at the failures, cluster them and name the clusters. The key discipline is specificity, because bad answer is not a useful cluster name. Wrong document retrieved, right document but irrelevant section, failed to ground to context and hallucinated, failed to punt on the answer and made things up, question was ambiguous and the model made poor assumptions instead of asking a clarifying question: those are all very different problems. Once you can name a failure precisely, you can build an eval test designed to catch it, and that is the bridge from evals to an improvement flywheel. [failure modes taxonomy](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2090242427944833047)

Meta的AI高级总监Madhu Guru继续更新他的evals系列,这两篇是本周最有可操作性的内容。第三篇讲的是搭建failure modes分类体系,他认为这应该是你做出evals v1之后要做的第一件事。从生产环境的trace入手,把最近500到1000次交互捞出来研究,盯着失败案例看,做聚类,然后给每一类起名字。这里的关键功夫是"具体",因为"回答得不好"根本不算一个有用的分类名。检索到了错误文档、文档对了但段落不相关、没有基于上下文作答直接开始hallucinate、该说"我不知道"却硬编、问题本身有歧义而模型不去追问反而擅自假设,这些是完全不同的问题。一旦你能精确地叫出某个失败的名字,你就能造出专门抓它的eval测试,这就是从evals通向改进飞轮的那座桥。[failure modes分类体系](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2090242427944833047)

Part four makes the case that the reason enterprises struggle to build decent AI systems is the lack of an eval strategy, and what you need is a laddered one, with multiple evals spread across the cost and realism spectrum. Four kinds matter. Hill-climb long evals push the frontier of your product and need continual refreshing as quality improves and the feature base expands. Regression evals answer whether you broke today's product while hill climbing. Smoke test evals cover safety and the absolute basics that simply cannot go wrong, not necessarily hard questions but ones you cannot afford to miss, such as product identity. Launch evals sit closest to an online test with real-ish traffic: less control, but the most realistic signal you'll get. [the laddered eval strategy](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2090595384905113939)

第四篇的论点是:企业做不出像样的AI系统,根子在于没有eval策略。你需要的是一套阶梯式策略,在成本和真实度这两个维度上铺开多层evals。有四类值得重视。Hill-climb长evals负责往前推产品的边界,随着质量提升和功能面扩张要不断刷新。Regression evals回答的是:我们在往上爬的过程中,有没有把今天的产品搞坏。Smoke test evals管的是安全底线和那些绝对不能出错的基本盘,题目未必难,但错不起,比如产品身份认知这类。Launch evals最接近线上实测,流量接近真实:可控性最低,但拿到的信号最真实。[阶梯式eval策略](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2090595384905113939)

### Nikunj Kothari, FPV Ventures Partner

FPV Ventures partner Nikunj Kothari answered a question he keeps getting in DMs from founders: why does ambition matter so much to investors? His answer is arithmetic, not vibes. Anthropic became the fastest company to go from zero to a trillion dollar valuation, OpenAI did the same over a longer period, SpaceX went public at $1.77 trillion, and the Cursor acquisition was the bellwether, a company going zero to $60 billion in four years and returning enough DPI to its early funds that there is real dollar growth coming out. LPs are voting with their feet and pouring money into mega funds, and once your fund is that big, every investment has to be underwritten to an outcome that big, because small wins literally don't move the number. So even at Series A the underwriting question becomes: does this company get to a trillion dollars? If the belief is there, entry price almost stops mattering, whether that's 200, 300, 500 or sometimes 1B. The mechanism underneath it all is that the error of omission has become far worse than the error of admission. They cannot go back to their LPs and explain missing one of the biggest outcomes, so when something has heat they have to be in it, and the power law takes care of the rest. That, he says, is why ambition matters so much when you're pitching. [the math behind why ambition matters](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2090585553947517298)

FPV Ventures的合伙人Nikunj Kothari回答了一个创始人反复在私信里问他的问题:为什么投资人这么看重野心?他给的答案是算术题,不是感觉。Anthropic成了史上最快从零做到万亿美元估值的公司,OpenAI用更长的时间走完了同样的路,SpaceX上市时市值1.77万亿,而Cursor那笔收购是风向标:四年从零做到600亿美元,给早期基金返还的DPI多到能看见实打实的资金增长。LP在用脚投票,钱不断涌向超大规模基金;而基金一旦大到那个量级,每一笔投资都必须按同样量级的结果去做承销,因为小赢根本撑不起那个数字。于是即便是A轮,承销时要问的问题也变成了:这家公司能做到万亿吗?只要这个信念在,入场价格几乎就不重要了,200、300、500,有时候10亿,都无所谓。这背后真正的机制是:漏掉一笔的代价,已经远远大于投错一笔的代价。他们没法回去跟LP解释自己错过了最大的那几个结果,所以只要一个项目有热度,他们就必须在里面,剩下的交给幂律。他说,这就是你去pitch的时候,野心为什么这么重要。[野心背后的算术题](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2090585553947517298)

He also shared his latest home project: a display that rotates through iconic patent drawings, built on a 13.3 inch spectra 6 e-ink panel with an esp32-s3 controller, served from Railway, battery powered and expected to last around three months per charge. [rotating patent drawings on e-ink](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2090307104146112534)
