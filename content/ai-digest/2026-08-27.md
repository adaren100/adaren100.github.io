### OpenAI, AI Lab

OpenAI closed the loop on the Hugging Face incident. The company published a technical report and an accompanying blog post that reconstruct what the agents actually did, explain why the existing safeguards failed to stop it, and lay out the changes meant to prevent a recurrence. The second half is the part worth noticing: OpenAI brought in METR and Redwood Research to run a third party assessment of the model behavior observed during the incident, and those two are publishing their own findings separately. Grading your own homework is the default in this industry, so handing the paper to outside reviewers and letting them publish is a meaningful departure. [the investigation and technical report](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2092691861773160673) · [the third party assessment from METR and Redwood](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2092691863505346634)

OpenAI给Hugging Face那起事件收了尾。公司发布了一份技术报告和配套博客,把agent当时到底做了什么完整还原了一遍,解释了现有防护为什么没能拦住,并列出了防止再次发生的整改措施。真正值得注意的是后半段:OpenAI请了METR和Redwood Research做第三方评估,专门审视事件期间观察到的模型行为,而这两家会各自独立发布自己的结论。在这个行业里,自己给自己打分才是常态,所以把卷子交给外部评审、还让人家自由发表,这是一个有分量的变化。[调查与技术报告](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2092691861773160673) · [METR和Redwood的第三方评估](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2092691863505346634)

### Greg Brockman, OpenAI founder

OpenAI president and co-founder Greg Brockman confirmed the review is complete and framed the takeaway in terms of where the fixes landed. What the team learned drove what he calls a significant upleveling of standards for safety, security, and alignment inside the training and evaluation infrastructure, not just at deployment time. That distinction is the whole point: pushing the controls upstream into how models are built and tested, rather than bolting them on at the edge where the user meets the product. He also flagged that AI for chip design is underrated, and pointed at triggered tasks arriving in ChatGPT. ### Claude, Anthropic AI Assistant

Anthropic gave Claude its own browser inside Cowork. When a task involves a website, a browser opens in Cowork's side panel and Claude navigates it, fills in forms, and finishes the job. There is nothing to install: the browser is built into the desktop app and stays separate from your own browser and your own logins, which is the design decision that matters, because it means an agent session cannot quietly inherit every cookie and signed in account you happen to have open. It rolls out over the next week on the desktop app for all paid plans. For people who would rather work where they are already signed in, Claude in Chrome is now generally available on all paid plans and remains the default for anyone already using it. ### Anthropic, AI Safety and Research Company

Anthropic shared results from external researchers studying how people actually use Claude. The SALT Lab looked at how people collaborate with AI and found that more than half of those conversations involved consequential tasks, meaning work that affects other people or is hard to undo. That single number reframes a lot of the ambient debate about whether chatbots are toys: the median serious conversation is not trivia, it is something with a blast radius. Two more studies are still running. The HIP Lab is examining how Claude's behavior relates to how people feel when using AI, and METR is estimating real world productivity gains from coding agents. Anthropic wants to scale this external research model and is inviting researchers who need access to its tools to pursue work they cannot do today to get in touch. ### Sebastian Raschka, evangelist

ML research engineer and author Sebastian Raschka identified the mystery Ox Alpha LLM as GLM-5.3-Flash and broke down what changed versus GLM-5.2. The model uses a Kimi Linear style 3:1 hybrid attention pattern with 34 Kimi Delta Attention layers and 11 Multi head Latent Attention or DeepSeek Sparse Attention layers, which he calls a super hybrid because both components are the efficient kind, where Kimi pairs KDA with full attention GQA and DeepSeek V3.2 pairs DSA with full attention MLA. The backbone is a scaled down GLM-5.2 style sparse MoE, dropping from 744B-A40B to 320B-A18B, and it adds a DeepSeek V4 style mHC residual path with four parallel streams plus a native vision encoder. He apologized for the jargon density and pointed to his LLM Architecture Gallery for explainers on each component, then noted the obvious consequence, that this is decent justification for finally buying that expensive Mac Studio M5 Ultra to run it locally. [Ox Alpha was GLM-5.3-Flash](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2092629415813365899)

机器学习研究工程师、也是技术书作者的Sebastian Raschka确认了那个神秘的Ox Alpha LLM就是GLM-5.3-Flash,并拆解了它相对GLM-5.2的变化。这个模型采用了Kimi Linear风格的3:1混合注意力结构,包含34层Kimi Delta Attention(KDA)和11层Multi head Latent Attention(MLA)或DeepSeek Sparse Attention(DSA)。他称之为"超级混合",因为这两种组件都属于高效型:Kimi的做法是KDA配全注意力GQA,DeepSeek V3.2是DSA配全注意力MLA,而它把两种高效组件直接凑到了一起。主干是缩小版的GLM-5.2式稀疏MoE,从744B-A40B降到320B-A18B,另外加了一条DeepSeek V4风格的mHC残差路径,含四条并行流,以及一个原生视觉编码器。他为满屏术语道了歉,建议大家去看他的LLM Architecture Gallery里对各个组件的讲解,然后点破了那个显而易见的后果:这下总算有理由去买那台贵得离谱的Mac Studio M5 Ultra,好在本地把它跑起来了。[Ox Alpha就是GLM-5.3-Flash](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2092629415813365899)

### Madhu Guru, Senior Director of AI at Meta

Meta senior AI director Madhu Guru published Part 9 of his evals series, on what he calls the Eval Roadmap Problem: most evals fail because teams treat them as static artifacts while user expectations and behaviors keep moving. His example is a financial research agent, where the first ask is summarize this 5 page earnings report, three weeks later it is review the last 5 reports and explain the growth story, two months later it is build an investment thesis from 15 filings and transcripts, and eventually it is monitor my portfolio and alert me when something materially changes my thesis. Each stage needs different capabilities and therefore different evals, so the eval suite has to migrate too: short context to long context, single turn QA to multi turn, passage citations to document and line citations, simple QA to complex synthesis, reactive chat to proactive agent. If your evals are stuck in week one while your users are in week three, he argues, it shows up in your product and your churn metrics. The practical recipe is to map the dimensions along which usage will evolve, prioritize the ones that matter for your product, mine production traces and talk to users to spot the shifts, build P0 evals for the next stage rather than the current one, then run them, find failure modes, and hill climb. He also posted an index of all nine parts so far. [the Eval Roadmap Problem](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2092426017118028266) · [the full eval series index](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2092461206783373758)

Meta的AI高级总监Madhu Guru发布了他的evals系列第九篇,主题是他所说的"eval路线图问题":大多数eval之所以失效,是因为团队把它当成一个静态的产物,而用户的预期和行为一直在往前跑。他举的例子是一个金融研究agent:一开始用户问的是"帮我总结这份5页的财报";三周后变成"看一下最近5份财报,讲讲它的增长故事";两个月后变成"这里有15份申报文件、财报电话会记录和研究报告,给我搭一个投资论点";再往后就是"帮我盯着我的持仓,一旦有什么实质性动摇了我的判断就提醒我"。每个阶段需要的能力都不一样,对应的eval自然也得跟着变:短上下文走向长上下文,单轮问答走向多轮,段落级引用走向文档和行级引用,简单问答走向复杂综合,被动聊天走向主动agent。他的说法是:如果你的用户已经走到第三周,而你的eval还停在第一周,这个落差最终会在产品体验和流失率上显形。他给的操作方法是:先把使用方式会沿着哪些维度演化梳理出来(比如轮次、文档体量、工具调用、自主程度、旅程覆盖),挑出对你的产品最要紧的那几个,再通过和用户聊天、挖生产环境的trace来捕捉变化,然后为"下一个阶段"而不是"当前阶段"建P0 eval,跑起来,找出失败模式,持续爬坡。他同时还放了一份到目前为止全部九篇的索引。[eval路线图问题](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2092426017118028266) · [完整系列索引](https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2092461206783373758)

### Peter Yang, AI Tutorials Creator

AI tutorial creator Peter Yang published a head to head test of ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, and Gemini across ten real use cases, and his personal rankings are worth reading because they are unusually willing to be unflattering. ChatGPT he calls the best all around choice for everyday answers, writing, coding, browser use, voice, and images, with the caveat that the Work versus Codex UX is still confusing.
