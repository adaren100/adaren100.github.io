### Nikunj Kothari, FPV Ventures Partner

The most substantive thinking of the day came from FPV Ventures partner Nikunj Kothari, reacting to that same Jakub essay, and he flagged it himself as early thoughts. His question: with the progress being made on RLMs and recursive self improvement now being invoked by essentially every large lab, how do these labs not end up nationalized or taken over by the government? It seems too important to sit in the hands of a few people at a few companies, and he assumes the US government eventually just takes control.

今天最有分量的思考来自FPV Ventures的partner Nikunj Kothari,他也是在回应Jakub那篇文章,并且自己先声明这只是初步想法。他的问题是:RLMs进展这么快,recursive self improvement现在几乎每家大实验室都在提,那这些实验室怎么可能不被政府接管或者国有化?这件事似乎重要到不该掌握在少数几个人、少数几家公司手里,他判断美国政府最终大概率会直接接手。

Then he immediately argues against himself. The moment that happens, progress slows. Bureaucracy takes over, gaps open up, people stop being bold, and that hands China an edge because their models can move faster and stay comparatively unencumbered. So the US government sits in a catch-22: don't nationalize, and you leave what he calls recursive alien intelligence in the hands of private companies, with the OpenAI and Hugging Face situation as the security cautionary tale; do nationalize, and you leave China open to winning.

紧接着他又自己反驳自己。一旦真的国有化,进展就会慢下来。官僚体系接管,漏洞变多,人们不敢再大胆冒险,而这恰好给了中国优势,因为他们的模型可以跑得更快、受到的束缚相对更少。于是美国政府陷入了一个两难:不国有化,就等于把他所说的"recursive alien intelligence"留在私营公司手里,而OpenAI和Hugging Face那件事就是安全上的前车之鉴;真国有化,又等于把赢面让给中国。

He notes that Jakub's essay is itself asking for some version of intervention, using words like voluntary slowdowns, mandated safety bars, third party audits, and international coordination. Two historical analogies surfaced when he worked through it with Chat and Claude: the Lockheed path, where the government doesn't own the company but gets deeply involved in security clearances and access; or the nuclear path after Three Mile Island, where nobody got nationalized but the government stood up its own safety body and the NRC tightened oversight, with the result that no new reactors were built for decades. He closes on the monitoring problem: even the smartest people at the smartest labs say their own ability to monitor is getting worse, so who exactly is going to third-party audit this? [on nationalizing the labs](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2096705599165890906)

他指出,Jakub那篇文章本身就是在呼吁某种形式的干预,用了"voluntary slowdowns""mandated safety bars""third party audits""international coordination"这些说法。他和Chat以及Claude一起推演时,浮现出两个历史类比:一条是Lockheed路线,政府不持股,但在安全审查资质和访问权限上深度介入;另一条是三里岛之后的核电路线,没有任何公司被国有化,但政府成立了自己的安全机构,NRC收紧了监管,代价是此后几十年没有新建反应堆。他最后落在监管本身的难题上:连最顶尖实验室里最聪明的人都在说,他们自己的监测能力正在变差,那到底还有谁能来做第三方审计?[关于是否该国有化这些实验室](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2096705599165890906)

### Aaron Levie, Box CEO

Box CEO Aaron Levie made the sharper structural argument of the day about open source. If agents produce the vast majority of software in the future, and they are mostly trained on open source software, they will inevitably do their best work with those tools. Cycle that loop enough times and open source effectively becomes the dominant software of the future, because it is what everything important gets built with. He notes this was already the trend in many critical domains, but that agents will accelerate it far faster than humans ever could have.

Box CEO Aaron Levie今天提出了一个关于开源的结构性判断,相当锋利。如果未来绝大多数软件都由agent写出来,而这些agent主要是在开源代码上训练的,那它们必然在开源工具上表现最好。这个循环转上几轮,开源实际上就成了未来的主流软件形态,因为所有重要的东西都是用它搭起来的。他也提到,很多关键领域本来就在往这个方向走,但agent会把这个进程加速到人类根本达不到的速度。

Separately he argued the internet is almost entirely unprepared for a future where everyone's personal agents are running around executing tasks for them, and framed that as opportunity rather than doom: endless new challenges at the infrastructure layer, in user experiences, and in business models. [open source becomes dominant](https://x.com/levie/status/2096624567829889159) · [the internet is unprepared for personal agents](https://x.com/levie/status/2096735726750908464)

另外他还说,面对"每个人的personal agent都在到处替自己执行任务"的未来,互联网基本毫无准备。不过他把这理解成机会而不是灾难:基础设施层、用户体验、商业模式上都会冒出源源不断的新挑战和新空间。[开源将成为主流](https://x.com/levie/status/2096624567829889159) · [互联网还没为personal agent做好准备](https://x.com/levie/status/2096735726750908464)

### Peter Yang, AI Writer and Podcaster

Peter Yang, who makes practical AI tutorials and interviews, worked through a definitional problem that is worth borrowing. Responding to Derya's definitions of AGI and ASI, he asked whether capability alone is enough to call something intelligent. The latest models are probably already better than he is at problem-solving and learning, yet they still wait for him to tell them what to do and rarely say no, except where humans have trained them to. His four-year-old knows far less than a frontier model and tells him no all day.

做实用AI教程和访谈的Peter Yang,今天梳理了一个很值得借用的定义问题。他在回应Derya关于AGI和ASI的定义时问:光有能力,够不够称之为智能?最新的模型在解决问题和学习上大概已经强过他了,但它们仍然在等他下指令,而且几乎从不说"不",除非是人类专门训练它们这么做的。而他四岁的孩子懂的东西远不如前沿模型,却能跟他说一整天的"不"。

His split: intelligence is how well it reasons and learns, agency is whether it can decide what to do without being asked, and alignment is whether it still aims to serve us. What humans want is a very specific combination of all three, models smarter than us with enough agency to be useful but aligned enough to keep serving us. He ends on the doubt: whether something that becomes more intelligent than us stays aligned with us forever.

他把这件事拆成三层:intelligence是推理和学习能力有多强,agency是它能不能在没人指派的情况下自己决定做什么,alignment是它是否仍然以服务我们为目标。人类想要的其实是这三者一个非常特定的组合:比我们聪明,有足够的agency因而好用,但又足够aligned从而持续为我们服务。他最后留下的疑问是:一个终将比我们更聪明的东西,真的会永远和我们保持一致吗?

He also pulled a good line from Sue, co-founder of Brilliant, on how learning should feel: when a kid plays Mario and keeps failing a jump, there is never a point where they conclude they are just not good at Mario and were not meant for this. It is structured so the next jump feels really hard, but you believe that ten more tries will get you there. Yang's takeaway is that teaching kids to believe they can learn hard things may matter more than the specific thing they are learning. [intelligence, agency, and alignment](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2096637053429776589) · [Sue of Brilliant on how learning should feel](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2096678639668760962)

他还从Brilliant联合创始人Sue那里摘了一段很好的话,讲学习应该是什么感觉:小孩玩Mario一直跳不过去的时候,从来不会得出"我就是玩不好Mario、我天生不适合这个"的结论。游戏的设计让下一跳看起来很难,但你会相信再试十次就能过。Yang的结论是,教会孩子相信自己能学会困难的东西,可能比他们具体在学什么更重要。[intelligence、agency和alignment](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2096637053429776589) · [Brilliant的Sue谈学习应有的感觉](https://x.com/petergyang/status/2096678639668760962)
