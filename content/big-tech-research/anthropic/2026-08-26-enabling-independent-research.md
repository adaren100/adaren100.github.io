**[Enabling independent research on how people use Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/research/enabling-independent-research)** — _Anthropic · Aug 26_

**Main takeaway:** Anthropic published results from a pilot that let three outside groups — Stanford's SALT Lab, Oxford's Human Information Processing Lab, and METR — design their own studies over roughly 250,000 real Claude.ai and Claude Code conversations each. Anthropic believes this is the first time external researchers have run public independent studies on an AI company's own usage data.

**Main methods:**
- **Anthropic Insights is the access mechanism.** Formerly named Clio, it's the privacy-preserving tool Anthropic's own teams use to analyze usage across millions of conversations; Anthropic ran the data collection on the researchers' behalf while each group did its own independent analysis.
- **The gap being closed.** Outside researchers previously had to choose between lab-published analyses (real usage, but answering the lab's questions) and public datasets (their own questions, but skewed toward casual use). Neither supports independent research on how AI is actually used.
- **Review rights were deliberately narrow.** Anthropic's contractual review covered only user privacy, information that could help people violate usage policies, Anthropic's confidential information, and research accuracy — the groups are free to publish findings even if inconvenient for Anthropic.
- **Scope of the data.** Each group worked over roughly 250,000 Claude.ai or Claude Code conversations from April–May 2026, and the aggregate data from every project is being publicly released.
- **A separate privacy audit was run.** Because Anthropic wants to scale the program, it additionally audited all data shared with third-party researchers to verify the privacy protections held, reported in an appendix.
- **Findings are only partly visible here.** The SALT Lab study covers what kinds of work people bring to AI, what roles humans retain, and where human-AI collaboration breaks down, but the fetched article text cuts off mid-sentence in that section, so the specific numbers from the three groups aren't captured in this summary.

**[Anthropic 头一回把真实使用数据交给外部研究者](https://www.anthropic.com/research/enabling-independent-research)** — _Anthropic · 8月26日_

**Main takeaway:** Anthropic 公布了一个 pilot 的结果：他们让三家外部机构（Stanford 的 SALT Lab、Oxford 的 Human Information Processing Lab，还有 METR）各自定题，在大约 25 万条真实的 Claude.ai 和 Claude Code 对话上做分析。Anthropic 认为这是外部研究者第一次拿 AI 公司自己的使用数据做公开的独立研究。

**Main methods:**
- **入口是 Anthropic Insights。** 就是以前叫 Clio 的那套隐私保护分析工具，Anthropic 自己团队平时也拿它扫几百万条对话。数据采集由 Anthropic 代跑，分析各家自己独立做。
- **补的是哪块空白。** 以前外部研究者只有两个选择：看实验室自己发的分析（数据是真实的，但回答的是实验室关心的问题），或者用公开数据集（问题自己定，但样本偏休闲用途）。两条路都撑不起对真实使用情况的独立研究。
- **审阅权限故意收得很窄。** 合同里 Anthropic 能过问的只有用户隐私、可能帮人钻 usage policy 空子的信息、Anthropic 的机密信息，以及研究准确性。结论对 Anthropic 不利，研究者照样可以发。
- **数据规模。** 每组用的是 2026年4月到5月大约 25 万条 Claude.ai 或 Claude Code 对话，三个项目的聚合数据这次也一并公开。
- **另外做了一次隐私审计。** 因为想把这个项目做大，Anthropic 把所有交给第三方研究者的数据又审了一遍，确认隐私保护确实守住了，结果放在附录里。
- **具体结论这次只看到一部分。** SALT Lab 那组研究的是人们把什么样的活交给 AI、自己保留哪些角色、协作在哪儿会崩，但抓到的正文正好在这一段中途截断了，三家的具体数字没能收进来。
