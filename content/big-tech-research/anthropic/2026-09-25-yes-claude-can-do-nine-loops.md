**[Yes, Claude can do Nine Loops](https://www.anthropic.com/research/yes-claude-can-do-nine-loops)** — _Anthropic · Sep 25_

**Main takeaway:** A guest post by physicist-turned-science-writer Matt von Hippel, who publicly challenged AI companies to crack a frontier problem in his old subfield of theoretical particle physics and saw it beaten a month later: Claude computed a nine-loop scattering amplitude in N=4 super-Yang-Mills.

**Main methods:**
- **Written by an outside challenger, not by Anthropic.** Von Hippel is a former amplitudeologist who now blogs weekly at 4gravitons.com. He set the challenge because he didn't trust his own read on AI and found the experts he does trust split between "superintelligence in a few years" and "LLMs are near a ceiling and won't do impressive physics."
- **He deliberately picked computation over new mathematics.** Progress in math comes from ideas, and nobody knows how hard an idea is to find until it's found. A computation felt like firmer ground for a test.
- **The challenge as posed: a frontier problem, on a budget.** He wanted a task that looked out of reach not because the method was unknown but because it seemed to need more computers and time than working researchers could get, then asked whether a smarter artificial researcher could do it on the same hardware anyway.
- **Why amplitudes are the stake.** Scattering amplitudes convert particle momenta and energies into reaction probabilities, which is how Large Hadron Collider results get checked against theory. A mismatch would be evidence for a new theory, potentially touching dark matter or the matter/antimatter imbalance.
- **"Loops" is the difficulty dial.** Amplitudes are hard enough that physicists almost always approximate, truncating at a fixed number of loops — a measure of how complicated the interactions between particles are allowed to get. Nine is the number Claude reached here.
- **What this summary can cover.** Only the post's opening section was captured by the fetcher, so the actual calculation, the compute budget it ran on, and how Claude was driven aren't reflected above.

**[九个 loop，Claude 还真给算出来了](https://www.anthropic.com/research/yes-claude-can-do-nine-loops)** — _Anthropic · 9月25日_

**Main takeaway:** 这是一篇客座文章，作者 Matt von Hippel 以前做理论粒子物理，现在写科普。他公开向几家 AI 公司下了一道挑战题，出自他自己的老本行，结果一个月后就被解掉了：Claude 在 N=4 super-Yang-Mills 里算出了九个 loop 的 scattering amplitude。

**Main methods:**
- **出题的是外人，不是 Anthropic 自己。** von Hippel 以前是搞 amplitudeology 的，现在每周在 4gravitons.com 上写博客。他之所以出这道题，是因为自己对 AI 的判断心里没底，而他信得过的那些行家又吵得厉害：一派觉得超级智能就在几年内，另一派觉得 LLM 快到天花板了，连拿得出手的物理工作都做不了。
- **他故意挑了计算题，不是数学新思路。** 数学上的进展靠 idea，而一个 idea 到底难不难找，找到之前谁也说不清。相比之下，计算量是实的，拿来当测试更踏实。
- **题目的设定：前沿问题，还得省着算。** 他要的是那种看着做不了、但卡点不在方法而在算力和时间的题，然后看一个更聪明的"人造研究员"能不能在同样的机器上硬把它做出来。
- **为什么 amplitude 这么重要。** scattering amplitude 负责把粒子的动量和能量换算成反应发生的概率，Large Hadron Collider 的结果就是靠它跟理论对账的。一旦对不上，那可能就是新理论的证据，往下能牵到 dark matter，或者宇宙里正反物质为什么不对称。
- **loop 数就是难度旋钮。** amplitude 实在太难算，物理学家基本都在做近似，算到某个 loop 数就截断，这个数衡量的是粒子之间的相互作用最多允许复杂到什么程度。这回 Claude 做到了九个。
- **这篇总结的边界。** 抓取只拿到文章开头那一段，所以具体怎么算的、用了多少算力、Claude 是怎么被驱动着跑完的，上面都没能覆盖。
